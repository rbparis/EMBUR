# EMBUR Founding Pilot Playbook

## The first market

Start with owner-operated HVAC and plumbing companies in the United States with:

- 2–20 employees
- at least 30 inbound leads or service requests per month
- an owner or office manager still handling follow-up manually
- jobs commonly worth $500–$10,000+
- missed calls, aging estimates, or inconsistent follow-up

Do not broaden the pilot until five companies in this segment have used the same lead-recovery workflow.

## The promise

EMBUR finds the customers and revenue a service business is about to lose, tells the owner what to do next, and records the money and time recovered.

Do not lead with “AI,” “CRM,” or “business operating system.” Lead with missed opportunities and measurable recovery.

## The founding offer

Offer the first five companies a 30-day Founding Partner Pilot:

- guided onboarding
- daily priority list
- missed-lead and overdue-follow-up review
- owner-approved actions
- weekly revenue-recovered and time-returned report
- direct access to the founder

Test a paid commitment of **$99 for 30 days**. If an owner will not pay, ask what proof or capability would change the answer. Do not promise a revenue result; promise focused setup, measurement, and support.

## Outreach message

Hi [Name] — I’m building EMBUR for HVAC and plumbing owners who lose time and revenue chasing missed calls, open estimates, and overdue follow-ups. It identifies who needs attention first and tracks what gets recovered. I’m looking for five local owners to shape the first working pilot. Could I ask you six questions about how your team handles leads today? No sales presentation—20 minutes by phone or video.

## Discovery interview

Ask these questions without demonstrating EMBUR first:

1. Walk me through what happens after a new call, form submission, or text arrives.
2. Where do leads or estimates most often fall through the cracks?
3. How do you decide who needs a follow-up today?
4. What happens when the owner or dispatcher gets busy?
5. How many open estimates or unanswered leads do you think exist right now?
6. What is a typical booked job worth?
7. What tools already hold customer, phone, calendar, and estimate information?
8. What would EMBUR need to prove in 30 days for you to keep paying?
9. Who else must trust or approve a tool like this?
10. Would you pay $99 for a guided 30-day pilot? Why or why not?

Capture exact phrases. The customer’s language should replace internal product language on the website.

## Pilot onboarding

For each company, record:

- current lead sources
- current follow-up process
- average job value
- number of active estimates
- response-time expectation
- owner’s preferred communication channel
- starting count and value of at-risk opportunities

Never combine pilot company data. Every record must remain scoped to its authenticated business.

## Weekly proof report

Report only measured values:

- opportunities identified
- total value identified
- actions approved
- actions completed
- customer responses
- appointments booked
- revenue recovered
- minutes returned
- owner-reported confidence in the priority list

Do not label estimated pipeline value as recovered revenue.

## Pilot success gate

Continue investing in this segment when at least three of five pilot companies:

- use EMBUR at least three days per week;
- approve or complete at least five actions;
- report that the daily priority is useful;
- record a customer response, appointment, or recovered revenue;
- agree to continue as paying customers.

If the gate fails, interview the owners again before building additional features.

## Daily founder scorecard

Work remotely in one focused block that respects physical limits:

1. Contact five prospective pilot owners.
2. Book or complete one discovery conversation.
3. Record one product insight in this document or the founder journal.
4. Complete one product change tied directly to an interview or active pilot.
5. Stop adding features that do not improve activation, recovery, trust, or retention.

The daily win is a completed customer-learning or revenue action—not hours spent coding.
